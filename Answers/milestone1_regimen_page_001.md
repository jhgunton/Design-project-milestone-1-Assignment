With three equal doses on days 0, 21, and 42, increasing the dose from 1.0 to 1.1 to 1.2 mg/kg reduced the day-63 living-cell fraction to 6.699%, 5.589%, and 4.766%, respectively. Higher doses produced greater cytoplasmic payload concentrations and more sustained cell killing. However, they also increased the peak concentration and AUC of released payload in blood, indicating greater systemic payload exposure. For the selected regimen, the peak was approximately 0.6601 ng/mL and the AUC was 0.426 µg·h/mL. Selecting the smallest effective dose meets the efficacy target while limiting additional payload exposure. These exposure measures are toxicity proxies in this model, however, rather than direct predictions of clinical toxicity.
